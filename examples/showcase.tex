\section{Mathematics}\label{sec:math}
Inline math such as $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$ or \(\norm{x}_2 \le \sqrt{n}\,\norm{x}_\infty\)
is rendered with KaTeX. Numbered equations can be referenced:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss}
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx = \sqrt{\pi}.
\end{equation}
Aligned systems:
\begin{align}
  f(x) &= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!} x^k \label{eq:taylor} \\
  \tr(AB) &= \tr(BA) \quad \text{for } A, B \in \R^{n \times n}
\end{align}
Unnumbered display math:
\[ \det\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = ad - bc. \]

Equation~\eqref{eq:gauss} is the Gaussian integral and \eqref{eq:taylor} is a
Taylor series; both live in Section~\ref{sec:math}.

\begin{definition}
A function $f\colon \R \to \R$ is \emph{convex} if
$f(tx + (1-t)y) \le t f(x) + (1-t) f(y)$ for all $x, y$ and $t \in [0,1]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Jensen]\label{thm:jensen}
If $f$ is convex then $f(\mathbb{E}[X]) \le \mathbb{E}[f(X)]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take a supporting line at $\mathbb{E}[X]$ and use linearity of expectation.
\end{proof}
